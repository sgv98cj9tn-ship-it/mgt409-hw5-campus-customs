Reflection My take

1. How would I evaluate the agents on each ticket, and why?

All tickets eventually got resolved, but at what cost? And resolved doesn't always mean the customer was served: ticket 101 ended with no tee for Tauhid and no purchase order queued, so "resolved" only meant nothing was waiting on a human.

2. For each ticket, how did Actual compare to my Expected plan?

Every actual first call matched my expectation, but the delegations, especially on 102 and 103, involved the Boss a lot, and I don't know if that was necessary.

In this app every agent runs the same model, but if the Boss ran on a higher-reasoning model, it would use up more tokens than the other agents, so the better design is to involve the Boss as little as possible while still making sure tasks finish correctly.

3. What would have been simpler as one agent with tools, and why?

Ticket 102 could have been resolved by one agent who's able to check the rent notice against the lease, see whether the rent is already paid, and see whether our cash covers it along with the other bills due. Involving the Facilities agent and the Accounting agent separately is much more complicated.

  • Facilities already had every tool 102 needed: get_lease, review_outgoing_payments, and request_human_approval. In the run (102 tab), Facilities checked the lease and the payments, then reported back. The Boss then sent Accounting to run review_outgoing_payments a second time and queue the $2,400 payment.
  • Part of that was my design: the Facilities prompt tells it to ask Accounting whether cash covers the rent "if the Boss hasn't already", so the extra hand-off was built in.
  • Little would be lost: a human still approves the payment on the human desk, so a second agent checking the same numbers doesn't add real safety. It just added calls: the Boss alone used 42% of 102's tokens.

Where I wouldn't use one agent: tickets 101 and 103 both end in a customer reply. Right now the agent writing that reply can't see costs, vendors, or rent at all. One agent with every tool could, and then only the prompt and the leak check would keep that information out of the message. So: one agent for internal-only tickets like 102, and a separate customer-facing agent whenever a customer gets a message.

4. Three new problems this agent team could solve with the tools I built

a. A restock request, as a purchase order

I could submit a ticket to restock what's short, and the agents find the vendor, figure out the cost, and queue a purchase order for me to approve. That would finish what 101 left hanging.

How the tools cover it: check_product finds what's short: the size S tee (0), the crest mug (0), and 12 size M hoodies for 103. lookup_vendor matches each to a vendor (Bulldog Print Co for apparel, 5 days; Elm City Gifts for mugs, 3 days). Accounting prices it from unit cost (12 hoodies × $22 = $264), checks cash with review_outgoing_payments, and queues it with request_human_approval as a purchase_order.

Why it's interesting: with $160 in checking (Cash tab), the approve route would refuse a $264 hoodie order for lack of cash. The team would have to propose a smaller order, like a few size S tees at $8 each, or wait for money to come in (5c).

b. Spotting a fake rent bill

Like if an email says rent is now $3,000, Facilities can check the lease, see it's $2,400, and flag it instead of paying. None of our tickets tested that.

How the tools cover it: get_lease returns lease 1: Elm City Properties, $2,400 a month. The Facilities prompt already says a notice "could be wrong or even fake" and not to prepare a payment for one that doesn't match. Instead of a payment, Facilities can queue an other approval asking a human to call the landlord. Even if a payment were queued, the approve route checks the amount against the lease and would refuse $3,000.

Why it's interesting: on 102 the notice matched (it even gave no amount), so this guardrail never fired. This ticket would test it on purpose.

c. Which bill to pay first

We only have $160 left, so Accounting could look at everything coming due in the next couple months and rank it, without paying anything. Kind of a manual version of the daily payments agent.

How the tools cover it: review_outgoing_payments with days_ahead=60 lists every unpaid bill and rent due in that window, the total cash, and any shortfall. Accounting ranks them by due date and consequence, and only queues payments for a human to approve.

Why it's interesting: run today, it would show invoice 501 and the September rent as paid and nothing else due, because the database only knows the next rent date. The October rent ($2,400, around 2026-10-02) isn't recorded anywhere yet. So this ticket works with today's tools, but it also shows exactly why the daily agent in 5a needs recurring-payment data.

5. Three new problems this agent team couldn't solve, and the tools and agents I'd need

a. Seeing payments coming before they're due

Something the agents can't do right now is anticipate things. Rent is a standing payment, but the team only reacts when a ticket arrives: rent showed up as ticket 102 just two days before it was due (2026-09-02), and paying it left $160 in checking.

Why they can't: nothing runs on its own. Every run starts when someone clicks "Send in the crew". No tool can create a ticket (agents can only read and update them), and leases has the rent and the next due date but no lease term or end date.

What I'd build: an agent that triggers automatically every day to check for payments due within the week or the month. It needs a daily scheduled job that starts it; the existing review_outgoing_payments with days_ahead set to 7 or 30; a new create_ticket tool so it can open a ticket for the team before a bill is due; and a recurring-payments table (lease term, end date, other repeating bills) so it can see further than what's already recorded.

b. Vendors asking about their orders

Any ticket where a vendor asks for the details of an order can't be resolved.

Why they can't: there's no orders or purchase-order table to look up, and the only message an agent can queue is a customer message. The leak check would even block a reply that names a vendor. On 101 the restock depends on Bulldog Print Co, but nothing records an order with them.

What I'd build: a purchase-orders table with a tool to look orders up and update them, a vendor_message approval type with its own send route (which doesn't apply the customer leak rules), and a Purchasing agent that owns vendor conversations, with Inventory and Accounting feeding it stock and cost facts.

c. Money coming in

Why they can't: cash only goes down. The approve route is the only thing that changes cash_accounts, and it only subtracts, so the Cash tab shows two outflows ($840 and $2,400) and no inflows. Nothing records a sale. On 103 I approved $49.30 a hoodie, but if Yale AI Club pays (8 in stock now = $394.40, or all 20 once restocked = $986.00), nothing in the system would know.

Why it matters: with $160 left in checking, money coming in decides whether the size S restock for 101 is affordable, and the daily payments agent in (a) would only have half the picture without it.

What I'd build: sales/orders and money-owed-to-us tables; record_sale, record_payment_received, and list_unpaid_customer_orders tools; a human confirming the money actually arrived before cash goes up, the same way a human confirms money going out; and a Receivables agent, or new tools for Accounting, that matches payments to orders and flags overdue ones.
